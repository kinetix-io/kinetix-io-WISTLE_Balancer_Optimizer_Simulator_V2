WISTLE: Work Instruction & Standard Time = Line Efficiency

Comprehensive Enterprise Manufacturing Optimization Engine

1. Introduction & Core Objective

WISTLE is an advanced, algorithm-driven enterprise simulation platform custom-engineered for LG manufacturing lines (Air Conditioner, Refrigerator, Washing Machine and Television Business Units). It converts raw production-line takt time data into an interactive digital twin of the assembly line, so Industrial Engineering and Production teams can analyze, rebalance and optimize manpower allocation and task routing before changes are made on the physical shop floor.

At its core, WISTLE answers three questions for any given model mix: (1) Where is the line currently bottlenecked? (2) What manpower or task-routing change would relieve that bottleneck? and (3) What would the resulting Line Balancing Efficiency, UPH/UPPH and Yield Rate look like if that change were applied? The application supports both a fully manual "what-if" workflow (drag-and-adjust manpower, move micro-tasks between stages, exclude/include stages) and an automated Auto-Optimize heuristic that iteratively rebalances breaching stages on its own.

Who this guide is for

This page is written for Line Balancing Engineers, Production Planners and IE Supervisors who will use WISTLE to review takt data, simulate manpower/task changes, and export audit-ready optimization records. No coding or database knowledge is required to operate the tool.

2. Key Terminology

  • Stage / Station: A single position on the assembly line (e.g. "S12: Barrier Assembly"), identified by its Stage ID. A stage can host one or more Micro-Tasks and is staffed by a Manpower (MP) allocation.
  • Micro-Task: An individual work element performed within a stage (e.g. "Pick & Fix Rail, Guide F"), each carrying its own Work Content (time in minutes) for a given model.
  • Manpower (MP): The number of operators allocated to a stage. Fractional values (e.g. 0.5, 1.5) represent shared/part-time staffing across stages.
  • Work Content (WC): The standard time (in minutes) required to complete a task or the sum of tasks at a stage, for a given model.
  • Actual Takt Time: The real cycle time a stage runs at, given its current Work Content and Manpower — see the formula in Section 8. This is the number WISTLE calculates continuously as you edit the line.
  • GERP Standard Takt Time: The target/benchmark cycle time for a model, sourced from the GERP Master Standard Time data and used as the STD Takt Benchmark on the Optimizer page.
  • Breach: A stage whose Actual Takt Time exceeds the STD Takt Benchmark — i.e. it is running slower than the target cycle time and is a candidate bottleneck.
  • Bottleneck: The single highest Actual Takt Time among all active stages. Because stages run in series on an assembly line, the bottleneck stage caps the maximum output rate of the entire line.
  • Line Balancing Efficiency (%): How evenly work is distributed across stages relative to the bottleneck — see Section 8 for the exact formula and a worked example.
  • UPH / UPPH: Units Per Hour and Units Per Person-Hour — throughput metrics derived from the bottleneck takt time.
  • Executed vs. Draft state: On the Optimizer page, edits you make (manpower changes, task moves, stage filtering) are held as a draft until you click Execute & Save. Only then do the calculated Takt values, chart and KPIs move — see Section 5 for the full explanation.

3. Getting Started

3.1 Sign in and Business Unit selection

Select your Business Unit (AC / RF / WM / TV) from the login screen, enter your Production ID and Password, and click INITIALIZE WISTLE. Every subsequent file upload, saved session and audit record is scoped to the Business Unit you signed in with, so the prefix (e.g. AC_) is validated against the file names you upload.

3.2 Resuming or starting a session

If you have previously saved a named session for this Business Unit, a Resume Workspace picker appears right after login, listing every saved session by name and save date. From here you can:

  • LOAD a listed session to restore its ingested data, Optimizer state and filters exactly as they were when saved.
  • DELETE a listed session you no longer need.
  • + START NEW SESSION to bypass all saved sessions and begin with a clean workspace.

If nothing has been saved yet for your Business Unit, WISTLE skips the picker entirely and takes you straight into a fresh workspace.

3.3 Saving your session at logout

Click LOG OUT in the top-right of the header at any time. If you have ingested data in the current session, WISTLE asks whether you want to save your workspace before signing out:

  • Choose Yes, then give the session a name (a suggested name with the Business Unit and timestamp is pre-filled) — it is added to your saved-session list for next time you log in.
  • Choose No to log out without saving.
  • Cancel the name prompt to abort the log-out entirely and stay signed in — nothing is lost.

You can keep any number of named sessions per Business Unit (e.g. "Line 3 – Peak Season Mix", "Line 3 – Baseline"), and switch between them freely from the login picker.

4. Dashboard Page Guide

The Dashboard tab is where you ingest the two source files that drive the whole application:

  • TAKTDATA (Actual Takt Time Data): the raw per-model, per-stage, per-task Manpower and Work Content extract from the line.
  • MASTER ST (GERP Takt Time Master Data): the GERP Standard Takt Time benchmark per model.

Both files must be named with your Business Unit prefix (e.g. AC_TaktData.csv, AC_MasterST.csv). Alternatively, switch to Fetch from DB to reload a previously archived snapshot instead of re-uploading CSVs. Click ↻ REFRESH DATA to ingest — WISTLE parses both files, cross-maps every Tool Group → Model → GERP Standard Takt Time, and renders:

  • Top-line stats: Tasks, Stages, Models, Total MP and Total Work Content ingested.
  • Line-level KPI tiles: Overload Stages, Bottleneck (Max Takt), Yield Rate, UPH/UPPH and overall Line Balancing %, computed across the raw ingested dataset (see Section 8 for formulas).
  • The Model Performance Ledger: a per-Tool-Group, per-Model breakdown of GERP Standard Takt, Allocated MP, Actual Takt and ST Efficiency % (GERP ST ÷ MP ÷ Actual Takt — can exceed 100% when the standard time is generous) — useful for spotting which specific models are driving a bottleneck before you move to the Optimizer.
  • A Raw Data Verification Preview (first 25 rows of each file) so you can sanity-check that the correct files were uploaded and columns mapped as expected.

Once ingestion completes, move to the Optimizer tab to select which Tool Groups/Models to simulate and begin rebalancing.

5. Optimizer Page Guide

5.1 Setting up the simulation

Choose the Tool Group(s) and Model(s) to include, set the STD Takt Benchmark (defaults to the GERP standard for the selected models, but can be overridden), then click Refresh Mapping to build the station-by-station simulation table and chart from the selected models' averaged Work Content and Manpower.

5.2 Draft edits vs. Executed results — read this before editing

The Optimizer separates what you are changing from what has been calculated. Any manual change — adjusting a stage's manpower, moving a micro-task to another stage, or filtering which stages are shown — is applied to your working draft immediately, so you can keep editing freely. However, the calculated Actual Takt values, the breach/no-breach coloring, the stacked-bar chart and the "Current" column of the KPI tracker deliberately do not move yet. Any stage whose draft no longer matches the last executed result shows an amber ⏳ PENDING badge in place of its Takt number.

When you are satisfied with your changes, click ▶ EXECUTE & SAVE (enabled only when there is something pending) to recalculate the Takt values, refresh the chart and KPI tracker, and commit the change to the Audit Trail in one action. This two-step "stage, then execute" model exists so that a batch of related edits — e.g. shuffling three tasks and adjusting two manpower values to relieve one bottleneck — is evaluated as a single coherent change, rather than showing you a flickering, partially-updated line balance after every individual click.

Auto-Optimize is the one exception: because it is already a single, self-contained automated action, it executes and commits its own result as soon as it finishes running, without needing a separate click on Execute & Save.

5.3 Manual editing tools

  • Manpower (MANUAL FEED): use the +/- steppers or type directly into the manpower field for any stage (0.5-person increments).
  • Micro-task routing: use the "Move" dropdown next to any micro-task to relocate it to a different stage.
  • Task lock 🔒: tick a micro-task's lock checkbox to exclude it from being moved by Auto-Optimize (manual moves via the dropdown are unaffected).
  • Stage filter (▼ icon in the STATION column header): an Excel-style filter — search, then tick/untick individual stages, or use Select All / Clear All — to focus the table and chart on a subset of stages.
  • HIDE IDLE: hides stages with zero manpower or zero work content. BREACHED (GRAPH ONLY): restricts the chart to stages currently breaching the STD Takt Benchmark.

5.4 Undo / Redo

Every manual edit — including manpower changes, task moves, stage filter changes, and a full Auto-Optimize batch — is snapshotted before it is applied. ↶ UNDO and ↷ REDO step backward/forward through this history (up to 40 steps), reverting both the draft table and, where applicable, the executed calculated results together.

5.5 Auto-Optimize

Clicking Auto-Optimize runs an iterative heuristic (up to 10 passes) that repeatedly finds the currently-breaching stage with the highest Takt, and attempts to move its lightest unlocked micro-task to a neighboring stage (or a stage within a custom range you define — see 5.6) if doing so would lower the bottleneck. It stops early once no further improving move can be found, or after 10 passes, and then executes/commits the result automatically.

5.6 Stage Shuffling Constraints

The panel next to the Stage table lets you constrain where Auto-Optimize (and, informationally, your own manual moves) may relocate each micro-task, by typing a Stage ID range (e.g. 5-20) against it. Use Submit Overrides to apply typed constraints, or use ↓ CSV / ↑ CSV to export the full constraint template, edit it in a spreadsheet, and re-import it in bulk.

6. Optimization Audit Page Guide

The Optimization Audit tab lists every executed change in the current session — manual and Auto-Optimize alike — with the source/destination stage, the manpower and Takt before/after, the resulting bottleneck, and the Line Balancing % delta for that step. Use ↓ CSV (Current Run) to export the current run's ledger, or ☁ SAVE RUN TO DB to archive it for later retrieval. Previously archived runs can be browsed and reloaded from the historical runs list on this page.

7. DB Dashboard Page Guide

The DB Dashboard tab is the systems/administrative view of WISTLE's storage layer — how many data snapshots and optimization runs have been archived, how much storage they occupy, and trend charts across archived runs. See the page itself for the live metrics; it is intended for IE leads and administrators auditing usage rather than day-to-day line balancing work.

8. Formulas & Calculation Logic

All calculations below are performed per active stage (i.e. stages currently included by the stage filter, and — where noted — the executed snapshot rather than unsaved draft edits), then aggregated across the line.

8.1 Actual Takt Time (per stage)

Actual Takt Time = Stage Work Content ÷ Stage Manpower

Where Stage Work Content is the sum of the standard times of every micro-task currently assigned to that stage.

8.2 Bottleneck

Bottleneck = MAX( Actual Takt Time ) across all active stages

8.3 Breach

Stage is Breached ⇔ Actual Takt Time > STD Takt Benchmark

8.4 Line Balancing Efficiency (%)

Line Balancing % = [ Σ(Stage Work Content) ÷ ( Σ(Stage Manpower) × Bottleneck ) ] × 100

The same definition is used everywhere in WISTLE, always at stage level: on the Dashboard (all models averaged), in the Optimizer KPI tracker and the Optimization Audit (stations kept by the stage filter and Hide Idle), and in the Predictive Planner (plan-weighted stations).

Intuitively: this is the ratio of total useful work being done to the total work capacity the line is paying for at the bottleneck's pace. A perfectly balanced line (every stage exactly at the bottleneck takt) scores 100%; any stage running faster than the bottleneck represents "wasted" capacity that pulls the score down.

8.5 UPH (Units Per Hour) and UPPH (Units Per Person-Hour)

UPH = 60 ÷ Bottleneck Takt Time (minutes) UPPH = UPH ÷ Σ(Stage Manpower)

These are capacity-based line metrics: what the line can produce at its bottleneck. LG's Manufacturing Indices use a plan basis (UPH = Plan Qty ÷ working hours; UPPH = Plan Qty ÷ attendance man-hours); the Predictive Planner shows both.

8.6 Yield Rate (LG Manufacturing Index)

Yield Man-Hours = Plan Qty × Weighted GERP ST (min) ÷ 60 Yield Rate (%) = Yield Man-Hours ÷ Attendance Man-Hours × 100 Attendance MH = (Direct MP + Indirect MP) × Working Hours × Days

Yield Rate needs a production plan (Plan Qty), so it is calculated only on the Predictive Planner. The Dashboard and Optimizer show it as N/A rather than an estimate.

8.7 Worked Example

Consider a simplified 3-station line with a STD Takt Benchmark of 1.80 minutes:

StageManpowerWork ContentActual TaktBreach?
S12.03.0 min1.50 minNo
S21.02.0 min2.00 minYes (bottleneck)
S33.04.5 min1.50 minNo
Bottleneck = MAX(1.50, 2.00, 1.50) = 2.00 min (Stage S2) Line Balancing % = (3.0 + 2.0 + 4.5) ÷ ( (2.0+1.0+3.0) × 2.00 ) × 100 = 9.5 ÷ (6.0 × 2.00) × 100 = 9.5 ÷ 12.0 × 100 = 79.17% UPH = 60 ÷ 2.00 = 30.0 units/hour UPPH = 30.0 ÷ 6.0 = 5.0 units per person-hour Breaching stages = 1 (S2)

To relieve the S2 bottleneck, an engineer would use the Optimizer to either add manpower to S2, or move part of its Work Content to a neighboring stage with spare capacity (e.g. S1 or S3, both currently at 1.50 min), then click Execute & Save to see the recalculated Bottleneck and Line Balancing %.

9. Tips, Best Practices & Troubleshooting

  • Nothing happens when I click Execute & Save: the button is only enabled once you have at least one un-executed manual change (an amber PENDING badge will be visible on the affected stage row).
  • My Takt/KPI numbers didn't move after a manual edit: this is expected — see Section 5.2. Click Execute & Save to apply the calculation.
  • Use Undo instead of manually reversing an edit — it correctly reverts both the draft and, where relevant, the last executed result together, whereas re-editing by hand can leave stray Audit Trail entries.
  • Name sessions descriptively (e.g. include the model mix or scenario) so they are easy to tell apart in the login picker later.
  • Filenames must carry your Business Unit prefix (e.g. AC_) or the CSV upload will be rejected — this prevents accidentally ingesting another line's data.
  • Lock a micro-task before running Auto-Optimize if it must never be moved (e.g. a task tied to fixed tooling at that stage).

11. Predictive Planner (Method A)

Choose the line and date range. The plan overview (units, models, work orders, days, and a daily volume vs required takt chart) needs only the GMES plan. Once TAKTDATA is loaded, each model's work content is blended by its share of the plan and the line is sized against the plan's Target Takt.

w_m = Q_m ÷ ΣQ WC_i = Σ_m WC_i,m × w_m ST_s = Σ WC_i (tasks in station s) Target Takt = (H × 60 × Days) ÷ Q_total MP_req = ⌈ TWC ÷ Target Takt ⌉ LBE_pred = TWC ÷ (MP_req × Target Takt) LBE_act = TWC ÷ (Σ MP_s × Takt_max) UPH = 60 ÷ Takt_max UPPH = UPH ÷ Σ MP_s Yield Rate = (Q × Weighted GERP ST ÷ 60) ÷ ((Σ MP_s + Indirect MP) × H × Days)

Open in Line Design passes the plan-weighted tasks, the plan quantity and the available time to the Line Design page.

12. Line Design (Workstation Allocation)

Line Design follows the standard line-balancing method for product layouts. It groups tasks into the fewest workstations that respect the task order (precedence) and the takt time, then reports idle time and line efficiency.

  1. Takt Time = Available time ÷ Units required
  2. Total Work Content = Σ task times
  3. Theoretical minimum workstations = Σ task times ÷ Takt, always rounded up
  4. Allocation: open a workstation with the full takt. From the tasks whose predecessors are all done and that still fit the remaining time, add the one with the largest time first (other rules can be selected). Close the station when nothing else fits. A single task longer than the takt gets its own station with ⌈time ÷ takt⌉ parallel operators and is flagged.
  5. Line Efficiency = Σ task times ÷ (Actual workstations × Takt) × 100. Balance delay = 100 − efficiency.
Illustrative example: 8 h = 480 min, 200 units, tasks A–G = 8.75 min Takt = 480 ÷ 200 = 2.4 min Theoretical = 8.75 ÷ 2.4 = 3.65 → 4 workstations Allocation: WS1 {A} · WS2 {B} · WS3 {C} · WS4 {E, D} · WS5 {F, G} → 5 workstations Efficiency = 8.75 ÷ (5 × 2.4) × 100 = 72.92 %

Tasks can come from a Task Precedence file, from the loaded TAKTDATA micro-tasks (time basis = plan mix, simple average or a single model; task order from the Precedence Master below). ↓ Tasks as Precedence Template exports the current tasks in the template layout so the IE team can relax the predecessors where tasks can be done in any order and upload the file again.

Precedence Master (TAKTDATA source)

When Line Design runs on TAKTDATA, the order the tasks must follow comes from the Precedence Master: versioned master data owned by Industrial Engineering. It holds one combined precedence diagram per business unit, line and tool group. Models of the same tool group share the diagram and differ only in task times, which come from TAKTDATA and the plan mix. With nothing saved, the current routing order applies.

  • Any order within stage: the micro-tasks of that stage may be done in any sequence once the stage can start.
  • Starts after stage(s): the stage(s) that must be finished before this stage can start, e.g. 22 or 22, 30. none means it can start at the beginning of the line; blank keeps the previous stage.
  • Task rules: for individual exceptions, list the task IDs a task waits for (e.g. T066, T070), or none.
  • Every edit is checked at once for unknown stages or tasks and for loops. Line Design immediately shows the effect of the edited rules.
  • Save New Version stores the rules for every tool group in scope with a mandatory reason and an effective date. Earlier versions are never overwritten; Version History shows who changed what and when, and can load any earlier version back into the editor.
  • When several tool groups are in scope, Line Design applies all of their rules together (a task must respect every one).
  • ↓ CSV / ↑ CSV exchange the rules with Excel for bulk editing.

Precedence Diagram

Below the workstation chart, the Precedence Diagram draws the line one box per stage, left to right. Arrows show which stage must finish before the next can start, and stages that can run in parallel appear on separate lanes. Each box is coloured by the workstation that carries most of its work and labelled with the workstation range. ⇄ marks a stage whose tasks may be done in any order. Click a stage to see its own micro-tasks, what they wait for and which stage follows. Fit to Width shows the whole line at once, and ↓ PNG saves the diagram for reports. Precedence files without a Current Stage column are drawn task by task when they have up to 60 tasks.
